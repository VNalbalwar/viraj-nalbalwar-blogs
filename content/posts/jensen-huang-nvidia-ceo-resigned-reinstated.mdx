Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO on November 23, eleven days after he resigned and six days after the board's chair said it did not intend to bring him back. In between, the company posted a record quarter, Huang said he would be honored to return, and the board said it had met with him and heard from senior parts of the US government. Here is the sequence, built from NVIDIA's own releases and the reporting around them.

## What Nvidia announced on November 12

NVIDIA's [announcement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm) said Huang had resigned as president, CEO and member of the board, effective immediately. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, took the interim CEO job while keeping her finance role, and Harvey C. Jones became the board's first-ever chair. The board said it was starting a search for a permanent CEO and would look at internal and external candidates. The announcement gave no reason for the resignation.

The context came from elsewhere. [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns/) reports that the board had announced an independent investigation days earlier, and that Huang told employees he took full responsibility. Several outlets reported on his personal conduct and on a foreign intelligence effort that reportedly targeted him. NVIDIA's later release refers to recent reporting that he had been the target of such an effort, and gives that as the reason for the investigation.

Some of those reports name private individuals. Fortune notes that neither Nvidia nor Huang has confirmed the relationship claims, so this post leaves the names and the details out.

The market reacted fast. SF Standard says the stock closed Wednesday at $173.73 and opened Thursday at $151.00.

## The week the board said no

November 17 packed three events into one day. Huang posted his first statement since resigning. According to [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/17/jensen-huang-honored-return-ceo-nvidia-board-approval-public-statement/), he said he would be honored to return if the board decided that was best for shareholders and the company, and he acknowledged serious mistakes in his personal life.

Hours later, on the earnings call, chair Harvey Jones said the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," according to [NPR](https://www.wusf.org/2026-11-17/nvidia-board-says-it-does-not-intend-to-reinstate-jensen-huang-as-ceo). Jones described the resignation as voluntary, following Huang's disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct.

That call also carried the numbers. NVIDIA's [results release](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027) for the quarter ended October 25 reports revenue of $115.8 billion, up 20% from the previous quarter and 103% from a year earlier. Data Center revenue was $107.6 billion, and GAAP net income was $65.3 billion. For the next quarter NVIDIA expects revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, and says that outlook assumes no Data Center compute revenue from China.

Outside the company, Fortune reports an open letter in his defense, saying he deserves due process, that had gathered more than 170,000 signatures, with the signers' affiliations not verified. It also reports that President Trump said on Monday he would not mind Huang coming back.

## What changed by November 23

On November 23 NVIDIA [announced](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director) that the board had voted unanimously to reinstate Huang, effective immediately. It gave two reasons. First, after a series of discussions and meetings with him, the board said it was satisfied with his candor and his ability to lead. Second, it said it had received input from senior levels of the United States government and was confident his reinstatement poses no national security risk. Kress returns to her role as CFO. Huang said he was "honored to return to NVIDIA."

[SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/11/23/jensen-huang-reinstated-as-nvidia-ceo-president/) adds that the reversal followed conversations with President Trump, who publicly welcomed it. Senator Elizabeth Warren called the move "reckless" and asked the administration to say which senior officials weighed in. The same report says Nvidia shares rose about 30% on Monday. I could not check that figure against exchange data.

The release also describes new structure. The board formed a committee of independent directors to oversee trade compliance and national security risk, and it is searching for a Chief Compliance Officer, a new role that would report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee.

## What is still open

- **The investigation.** The release says the independent committee's investigation will proceed to conclusion. It gives no timeline and no findings.
- **Regulators.** SF Standard, citing the Wall Street Journal, reports that the SEC has opened its own inquiry into whether investors were misled. NVIDIA's release says only that it will keep cooperating with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.
- **Congress.** Fortune and NPR report that Huang and the company's general counsel are due for closed-door questioning by House committees on December 1 and 2. SF Standard adds that Huang will appear on December 3 at a live-streamed Senate Banking Committee hearing on AI chip export controls.
- **The reversal itself.** The board went from "no plan to reinstate" to a unanimous vote in six days. The release points to the meetings and the government input as the reasons.

For anyone building on Nvidia hardware, the operating facts in the filings are steady. On November 12 the chair said he expected operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption. The November 17 guidance assumes no China Data Center compute revenue. My read: the dates worth marking are the December hearings, because that is where the open questions about export compliance get asked in public.
