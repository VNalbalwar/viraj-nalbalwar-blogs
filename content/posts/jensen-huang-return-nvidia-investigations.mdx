A day after Jensen Huang got his job back as Nvidia CEO, he apologized to staff, the shares gave up 5.9%, and the inquiries around him and the company were still open. Getting reinstated answered who runs Nvidia. It did not end the investigations. If you missed how he got back in, [here is the 11-day sequence](/posts/jensen-huang-nvidia-ceo-resigned-reinstated).

## He apologized at a closed staff meeting

The meeting happened on Tuesday, November 24, and lasted about an hour. [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-slips-as-investors-assess-risks-surrounding-huangs-return-as-ceo-5047757), citing one person familiar with the matter, reports that Huang told employees he had violated parts of the company's code of conduct and made "some terrible mistakes." The same person said Huang rejected the idea that pressure from President Trump on the board got him reinstated.

[NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/jensen-huang-says-s-thankful-second-chance-nvidia-employee-meeting-rcna611235) has a similar account from an employee who attended. By that account, Huang expressed contrition, insisted that employees follow the code of conduct, and received a standing ovation. Each of those two accounts comes from a single person. The quote does have other support: The Next Web reports that The New York Times, which reviewed a livestream of the meeting, also has Huang saying "I made some terrible mistakes."

## The shares gave some back on a rough day

Monday was the rally: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-return.html) puts the jump at 31%. On Tuesday, Reuters reports, Nvidia closed 5.9% lower, and it remained almost 31% below its October 6 record high.

Context matters here. [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/jensen-huang-apology-nvidia-staff-therapy-shares-fall), citing The Information, reports that AMD fell nearly 9% the same day, while CoreWeave and Nebius, two cloud companies that rely on Nvidia, lost around 11%. The Next Web also has the Nasdaq 100 down 1.99%.

## What is confirmed, and what is only reported

**California** is the clearest case, because the state announced it itself. On November 19, Attorney General Rob Bonta [served Nvidia an investigative subpoena](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-serves-investigative-subpoena-nvidia). His office is asking whether the company's advanced chips or confidential information may have gone to China, and whether Nvidia misled investors and the public. The release says the answers will help decide whether California law was broken. It announces no findings. Bonta's summary of the principle: "Companies cannot mislead their investors."

**The board** is also on the record. Its [November 23 announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director) says the investigation by a committee of independent directors will proceed to conclusion, and that Huang supports it and will cooperate. The same announcement adds two pieces of oversight: a National Security and Export Control Committee made up of independent directors, to watch trade compliance and national security risk, and a search for a Chief Compliance Officer, a new job that will report to the board's Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee.

That is also the inquiry one analyst singled out. CNBC reports that StoneX analyst Cody Acree called it the "key risk" that remains.

**The SEC** is a different story. [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/embattled-nvidia-faces-sec-probe-as-california-attorney-general-mulls-action/269054) relays a November 17 Wall Street Journal report, based on people familiar with the matter, that the agency is opening a civil probe examining in part whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors. The article carries no confirmation from the SEC or from Nvidia. Reuters separately says Huang and Nvidia face state and federal probes and reviews, including from the FBI, the Justice Department and the SEC, without detailing them.

## What Washington said on Tuesday

Attorney General Todd Blanche told a press conference that "Nvidia is a good corporate citizen," The Next Web reports, and he would not say whether Huang had been investigated. Reuters reports that President Trump told the New York Post he would pardon Huang if necessary but believes Huang should be fine without one. Trump also said he is open to the US government taking an equity stake in Nvidia. Neither man announced the result of any inquiry listed above.

## The next two dates

On December 1, Huang is due to sit for a closed-door, transcribed interview with the House Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and the House Intelligence Committee, [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/17/jensen-huang-honored-return-ceo-nvidia-board-approval-public-statement/) reported before his reinstatement. On December 3, he is due to testify at a public Senate hearing. [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/11/23/jensen-huang-reinstated-as-nvidia-ceo-president/) says it is before the Senate Banking Committee, on export controls for AI chips.
